Clause-by-Clause Indian Law Compliance Audit

Scanner: Indian Contract Act 1872 & Arb. Act 1996
Clause 14.2: Limitation of Liability & Indemnity Carve-Out dangerous High Risk (Uncapped)
Page 28, Para 3
“...Neither party’s liability shall be limited with respect to indirect, punitive or consequential loss resulting from vendor negligence, and Supplier agrees to fully indemnify Bharti Hexacom without any financial aggregate cap whatsoever...”
report_problem Statutory Conflict: Consequential damages without an aggregate cap exceed customary judicial limits under Section 73 of the Indian Contract Act 1872 (remote losses inadmissible). Threatens vendor balance sheet and triggers cross-claim un-insurability.
Clause 22.4: Post-Termination Restraint on Key Personnel (Non-Compete) gavel Statutory Violation (Void Ab Initio)
Page 39, Para 6
“...For a period of 24 months following the termination of this Agreement, Indus Towers shall not directly or indirectly engage in, contract with, or tender for any competing 5G tower infrastructure rollouts within the telecom circles of Delhi NCR, Mumbai, and Punjab...”
warning Precedent Authority: Under Section 27, Indian Contract Act 1872, post-employment restrictive covenants are non-severable and wholly void (Niranjan Shankar Golikari v. Century Spg. & Mfg. Co. & Percept D’Mark (India) v. Zaheer Khan).
Clause 26.1: Dispute Resolution & Unilateral Arbitrator Appointment block Critical Defect (Non-Enforceable)
Page 44, Para 1
“...All disputes shall be referred to a Sole Arbitrator appointed unilaterally by the Managing Director of Bharti Hexacom Ltd. whose determination shall be conclusive and binding upon both entities...”
balance Supreme Court Ruling: Supreme Court bench in Perkins Eastman Architects DVM v. HSCC (India) Ltd. (2020) and Section 12(5) read with Seventh Schedule of Arbitration Act 1996 explicitly prohibits interested parties from appointing a sole arbitrator.
Clause 11.3: Liquidated Damages vs Inchoate Penalty check_circle Verified Indian Law Standard
Page 22, Para 4
“...Failure to meet site uptime availability SLA of 99.95% yields pre-estimated liquidated damages at 0.5% per week capped at 5% of monthly recurring fee, agreed as a genuine pre-estimate of loss...”
verified Fully compliant under Kailash Nath Associates v. DDA (2015) 4 SCC 136 and Section 74 of the Indian Contract Act 1872. Reasonable pre-estimate without penal forfeiture.
