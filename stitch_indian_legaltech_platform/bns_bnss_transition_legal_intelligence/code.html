Harmonized Ratio Decidendi Matter Reference: CBI v. Vikas Mishra (Appln.)

The Supreme Court of India in Central Bureau of Investigation vs. R. Singhania & Ors. [(2024) 6 SCC 412, Paras 18–22] explicitly interpreted the structural departure of Section 187(3) of Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS) 2023 from the legacy regime of Section 167(2) of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973.

Under the newly enacted statutory framework, the maximum 15-day police custody remand is no longer restricted strictly to the first 15 days following initial detention. Instead, police remand can now be sought in fragmented intervals or tandem across the entire 40-day (for offences triable by lower courts) or 60-day (for heinous/economic offences) pre-charge-sheet window.

"Para 21: The legislative intent behind replacing Section 167(2) CrPC with Section 187(3) BNSS is explicitly designed to dismantle deliberate hospitalizations and evasive manoeuvres during the formative fifteen days of custodial investigation, particularly in multi-layered economic offences."

However, the Court cautioned that default statutory bail under Section 187(5) BNSS remains an indefeasible constitutional right flowing from Article 21, and the expiry of 60/90 days without a formal Police Report under Sec 193 BNSS mandates instantaneous release upon furnishing solvent sureties.
